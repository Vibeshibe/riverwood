MATRIX · PRIVACY

Private rooms.
Open protocol.

Matrix is a federated messaging network. This notice explains what the Riverwood homeserver processes, what encryption protects, and where copies of messages can travel.

At a glance: encrypted rooms protect message and file contents from the homeserver, but account, device, room-membership, timing, and routing data remain visible. Unencrypted room content is readable by the homeserver. Federated rooms copy events to other participating homeservers.

What the homeserver processes

Account and profile

Your Matrix user ID, display name, avatar, account-creation time, account status, linked Mastodon identity, and any profile information you choose to provide.

Sessions, devices, and encryption

Access and refresh tokens, device identifiers and names, session dates, last-seen information, IP addresses, user-agent information, public device and cross-signing keys, one-time keys, and encrypted key backups if you enable them.

Rooms and communications

Room memberships and invitations, room names and settings, power levels and other state events, messages, edits, reactions, redactions, files, voice messages, call-signalling events, read receipts, typing and presence information, reports, and moderation records. What is visible to the server depends on whether a room uses end-to-end encryption.

Technical logs

Request time, IP address, requested endpoint, response status, user agent, and diagnostic or security events. Ordinary technical logs are retained for 20 days.

Signing in with Mastodon

Riverwood uses the Mastodon service at social.riverwood.town as its single sign-on provider. During sign-in, Matrix receives the stable account identifier and the profile claims configured for login, such as username, display name, or avatar. Matrix stores the external identifier so future sign-ins return you to the same Matrix account.

Your Mastodon password is entered on the Mastodon service and is not provided to the Matrix homeserver. Matrix creates its own session and device tokens after successful authentication. Deleting or disabling one account does not necessarily delete the other; contact privacy@riverwood.town if both should be removed.

Messages, files, and encryption

End-to-end encrypted rooms

In an encrypted room, compatible Matrix clients encrypt messages and files before uploading them. Riverwood stores and relays ciphertext and cannot normally read the content without a decryption key. Private keys remain on participating devices. If you enable secure key backup, the homeserver stores an encrypted backup that can be unlocked only with your recovery key or recovery method.

Encryption does not hide the information needed to operate Matrix. The homeserver still processes your Matrix ID, participating devices, room membership, event type and size, timestamps, delivery routing, and other protocol metadata. Room participants who possess the keys can read and copy decrypted content.

Unencrypted rooms

Messages and files in rooms without end-to-end encryption are stored in readable form. They are visible to participants according to the room’s access and history settings and are technically accessible to the controller. Administrative access is limited to what is necessary for maintenance, security, abuse handling, legal obligations, and data-subject requests.

HTTPS protects data while it travels between clients and Riverwood, but HTTPS alone does not make a room end-to-end encrypted. Check the room’s encryption indicator before sharing sensitive information.

Federation and connected rooms

Matrix federation lets people on different homeservers join the same room. If a room contains a member from another homeserver, Riverwood sends that homeserver the room events it needs to maintain its copy. Depending on the room and encryption settings, this can include your Matrix ID, display name, avatar, membership and state events, messages, files, reactions, and encrypted event content.

Remote homeservers are operated independently and may keep their copies under different rules. Riverwood sends redactions and erasure-related updates through the protocol but cannot guarantee that every remote server or client deletes an existing copy. Remote homeservers can be located outside the European Union or European Economic Area.

Bots, bridges, widgets, integrations, and third-party clients can receive additional data when you add them, use them, or join a room containing them. Their operators act under their own privacy terms. If you want to avoid federation or third-country replication, use rooms containing only Riverwood accounts and do not add bridges, bots, or external widgets.

Purposes and legal bases

Providing Matrix
Account creation, authentication, rooms, messaging, media, encryption support, synchronisation, and federation are processed under Article 6(1)(b) GDPR to provide the communication service you request.
Security and moderation
Logs, rate limits, abuse reports, moderation records, and measures against spam or attacks are processed under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interests are protecting users and maintaining a secure, reliable service.
Legal duties
Data may be preserved or disclosed where required under Article 6(1)(c) GDPR or where necessary to establish, exercise, or defend legal claims.
Contact
Messages sent to Riverwood are handled under Article 6(1)(b) GDPR for account or service matters and Article 6(1)(f) GDPR for other enquiries.

A Matrix ID, linked authentication identifier, session and device data, room membership, and the events needed for requested communications are required to provide the service. Without them, Riverwood cannot create or operate a Matrix account. Display names, avatars, presence, read receipts, and similar profile or convenience features are optional where the client provides a setting.

The Matrix service does not use personal data for advertising, visitor profiling, or automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.

Retention, redaction, and account deletion

Account and profile data are retained while the account is active. Room events and uploaded media do not have a general fixed expiry unless a room-specific or server retention policy applies. Matrix state events, including membership and room administration history, may need to remain to preserve the integrity and permissions of a room.

Redacting a message removes most of its content from the event served by cooperating homeservers, while a reduced event remains in the room history. Redaction cannot remove screenshots, downloads, quotations, notifications, or copies already held by participants or non-cooperating remote systems.

When an account is deactivated, active sessions and devices are revoked, the account leaves joined rooms, and account data and encryption keys held for the account are removed where supported. If erasure is requested, the profile is anonymised and the account is marked erased. Previously sent messages and files are not automatically deleted from room histories, and existing participants may retain access to their copies. Uploaded media and the external sign-on mapping may require a separate erasure request.

To deactivate the account and request erasure, use the account-management option offered by your Matrix client or email privacy@riverwood.town. Technical logs are normally deleted after 20 days; records connected with an incident or legal claim may be retained until the matter is resolved. Operational backups, where present, are isolated for disaster recovery and overwritten through the ordinary backup rotation.

Recipients and international transfers

Data is available to the room participants and devices permitted by each room’s configuration. Federated room data is also received by the operators of every participating homeserver. Bots, bridges, widgets, push services, or other integrations receive data only when the relevant feature or room uses them.

Riverwood is self-hosted in Germany and does not use a separate hosting processor for the homeserver. Federation can nevertheless send data repeatedly to independently operated homeservers anywhere in the world when this is necessary to deliver the federated conversations you choose to join. Riverwood cannot select the location of another participant’s homeserver or provide contractual safeguards on its behalf. Consider this before joining a federated room or sending personal information.

Data may also be disclosed to public authorities where Riverwood is legally required to do so, or to legal and technical advisers where necessary to protect the service or handle a claim.

Your rights and contact

Where the legal requirements are met, you may request access (Article 15 GDPR), rectification (Article 16), erasure (Article 17), restriction (Article 18), and data portability (Article 20). Where processing is based on legitimate interests, you may object on grounds relating to your particular situation under Article 21 GDPR.

Send requests to privacy@riverwood.town. Riverwood may need to verify that you control the affected account. Protocol and federation constraints may limit erasure from copies held by other participants or independent homeservers; those limits will be explained when a request is handled.

You may complain to the Sächsische Datenschutz- und Transparenzbeauftragte, Maternistraße 17, 01067 Dresden, or another competent supervisory authority.
